Contract-window expansion protocol
Status: proposed follow-up; no additional model evaluation has been run.

Purpose
Add intermediate and more distant contract windows to describe the curvature
and saturation of the service-rights value curve. The current verified 12 h,
16 h and 24 h results establish two paired contrasts; they do not identify the
full response curve or an optimal contractual deadline.

Existing matching assets
The Eagle archive has current-model results for 12 h, 16 h and 24 h only.
Seeds/checkpoints are 20260920/epoch06, 20260921/epoch02, 20260922/epoch00.
The 30 h and 48 h Frontier records belong to a different workload trace and
must not be inserted as Eagle points on the same contract curve.

First extension: 8 h and 20 h
Use the same three checkpoints, 2025 forcing/admissions, reference book,
static electrical and compute caps, fee terms, no migration and terminal rule.
The original planner has a 96-slot (24 h) forecast horizon. Keep that horizon
fixed. The existing implementation can represent 8 h and 20 h deadlines by
extending its command-line choices while retaining its fixed state dimensions.
No retraining or forecast-horizon change is needed for these two windows.
Before accepting a point, export its complete book, original admissions hash,
checkpoint hash, quarter-hour settlement, deadline audit and terminal liability.
Name these new evaluations by their actual run date; do not call them restored
historical observations. Repeat the original 12/16/24 settings only if a code
change affects shared executor behavior, rather than duplicating checks.

Longer extension: 36 h and 48 h
The current program has a 96-slot queue and 96-slot state/forecast tensor. Merely
adding 36 or 48 to the CLI choices would index outside its queue; it is not a
valid protocol. A longer contractual queue must be represented explicitly.
Declare the treatment of known obligations beyond the 24 h forecast horizon,
including tail capacity, terminal cost and deadline guards. One defensible
protocol keeps the 24 h predictive horizon and uses an explicitly defined,
unchanged conservative continuation rule for later obligations. Another changes
the forecasting/planning horizon, but then that design change must be isolated
and reported, not interpreted as a pure contract-rights effect. Decide and
freeze the rule before evaluating the new windows. Additional state bins or
forecast extrapolation may change what the neural policy receives and must be
documented. Retain the same actual admissions and monetary account throughout.

Computational planning only
Archived current-model evaluations take approximately 228 to 287 seconds per
annual seed/run in their recorded hardware environment. Six extra 8/20 h runs
therefore correspond to roughly 23 to 29 minutes at that old observed rate,
excluding setup and audit. This is not a benchmark or guarantee for the current
machine. The 36/48 h extension needs implementation work before a runtime can
be estimated. No long-running evaluation was started for this revision.

Reporting
Display raw historical and newly evaluated points with their true provenance.
Report same-seed increments and all three seed results, preserving the common
calendar interpretation. Keep policy outcomes, perfect-information upper/lower
bounds, and ex-ante contract value separate. A denser policy curve alone does
not certify optimal contract value for the new windows.
