PROPOSED MAIN-TEXT SECTION: MATHEMATICAL CERTIFICATES AND NUMERICAL STATUS

Theoretical validity and numerical verification are separate statements. Fix
the admissions, initial cohorts, physical limits, reference account and terminal
rule, collected in theta. Let X_W(theta) denote the original feasible schedules
under a W-hour right and let J_theta(x) be their original total cost. A relaxation
P_W is valid only if every x in X_W has an embedding T(x) in P_W and its relaxed
cost satisfies J_rel(T(x)) <= J_theta(x). Consequently, a verified lower cost
bound L_W for P_W implies the savings ceiling U_W=C0-L_W for the original
contract. This implication does not require a relaxed optimum to be executable.

For the nominal wholesale account, the exact slot cost is

    f_t(x)=Delta t [p_t G_t(x)+V_t(x)+|G_t(x)-G_t^0|],
    G_t(x)=[PUE_t(Pidle+kx)-PV_t]_+,
    V_t(x)=[PV_t-PUE_t(Pidle+kx)]_+.

The zero-import point, reference-import point and both capacity endpoints
partition the full service interval into affine pieces. Thus the global minimum
of f_t(x)-lambda_t x is obtained by evaluating those exact breakpoints, including
negative-price intervals. It is unnecessary to assume that f_t is convex.

For a homogeneous D=4W-slot contract with zero initial backlog, write
x_t+q_t-q_(t-1)=a_t and 0<=q_t<=Q_t=A_t-A_(t-D+1), where A_s=0 for s<=0.
The terminal cost is tau q_N. For arbitrary equality multipliers lambda and
lambda_(N+1)=0, weak duality gives the original-cost lower bound

    L_W(lambda)=K+sum_t [lambda_t a_t
      +min_(0<=x<=c_t)(f_t(x)-lambda_t x)
      +Q_t min{0,lambda_(t+1)-lambda_t+tau 1_(t=N)}].             (C1)

A fixed nonzero initial backlog contributes lambda_1 q_0 and retains its own
deadline requirements. A fixed closing queue is represented by a singleton
bound. A nonlinear terminal rule requires its own exact box minimum or a proved
minorant; it cannot silently be dropped. Minimisation over independent service
and backlog boxes can only lower the Lagrangian, proving (C1).

The independent epigraph formulation admits the general finite-box proof. For
min c0+c'y subject to Ay=b, My<=h and l<=y<=u, let lambda be unrestricted and
mu>=0, and define

    r=c-A'lambda+M'mu,
    L=c0+lambda'b-mu'h+sum_i min{l_i r_i,u_i r_i}.                (C2)

On any feasible point, the equality multiplier term vanishes and
mu'(My-h)<=0. Minimising the resulting affine expression over its finite box
proves (C2), including nonzero lower bounds and imperfect stationarity. After
clipping inequality multipliers to their permitted signs, every term is
recomputed. A minimisation incumbent is not a lower bound. Facet dominance is
checked at every original breakpoint and endpoint; affine interpolation then
proves dominance between them. The finite epigraph bounds must retain the
embedding of every original feasible schedule. Original terminal liabilities,
common charges and any omitted physical coupling require the same containment
and cost-domination checks.

All stored binary64 coefficients used by the verifier are interpreted as their
exact binary rationals. Each complete exact scalar term is rounded downward
before accumulation, with a recorded total arithmetic loss. Any proved input
or model allowance is subtracted separately. If the baseline is enclosed by
[C0_minus,C0_plus], use U_W=C0_plus-L_W. A feasible candidate cost upper bound
J_B_plus gives H_B=C0_minus-J_B_plus. Savings ceilings round upward; cost floors
and witness savings floors round downward. With two valid cost bounds, the
tighter ceiling is min(U_W^(1),U_W^(2)); exclusion simultaneously against both
requires H_B>max(U_W^(1),U_W^(2)).
