Recent economic studies make clear that flexibility value depends on contractual and market institutions. Van de Sande Araujo et al. [12] show how complete bilateral flexibility contracting can recover look-ahead benefits in sequential market clearing. Biswas et al. [13] explain persistent day-ahead premia through disruption risk, limited arbitrage and generators' strategic commitments. Fei et al. [14] study direct and intermediated demand-response procurement, showing how channel competition can reduce double marginalisation. These studies identify mechanisms governing market coordination, implicit remuneration and procurement surplus. Their economic objects differ from the value of relaxing a particular service obligation on a fixed settlement path: an equilibrium premium or procurement gain cannot be inserted directly as that contract's monetary opportunity increment.

Our contribution is a verifiable identification procedure for this narrower object. With admitted service, equipment, realised prices, the reference and closing-liability account held fixed, a feasible expanded-rights value above a narrower-rights global ceiling certifies a minimum gain unavailable to every narrower-rights controller. The procedure separates rights-created opportunity from the portion captured by a fitted policy, and gives explicit monetary error allowances rather than treating solver tolerances as economic evidence. Its fixed-price scope does not identify equilibrium prices, welfare effects or a negotiated contract price. Instead, it supplies a contract-specific value interval that a procurement analysis can combine with separately established information constraints, tenant costs and participation requirements.
